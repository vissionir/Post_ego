---
title: Scope of the research
---

Different religions, philosophies, and practices describe the same content in different languages.

They are talking about the same phenomena: “I”.

The problem is not that these descriptions differ, but that they usually exist in isolation. The links between them are rarely held, which leaves understanding fragmented.

Post-Ego records the connectedness discovered between these descriptions — not as a new theory and not as a synthesis of traditions, but as a structure of correspondences between different ways of speaking about the same experience.

What traditions called samadhi, presence, nonduality, or awakening is approached here outside the language of traditions — through the links between experience, perception, and action.

The subject of the project is not a teaching and not a method, but a map of connections that makes it possible to study self and world as a single process, without relying on faith and without attaching to a system.

Ultimately, this is an inquiry into the nature of “I”: not as a personality or an image, but as that from which experience is lived at all.

The research is based on long-term self-analysis and metacognition, as well as a comparison of descriptions from different traditions and disciplines: attention and presence practices (in a secular Buddhist framing), NLP and work with inner structures, and philosophical and phenomenological models.

These sources are used not as systems or authorities, but as different languages for describing the same experience.

## Boundaries of the project

Post-Ego is not:

- a spiritual teaching;
- an esoteric system;
- a “awakening” practice or “state attainment” practice;
- a method of salvation, development, or personal transformation.

No belief, path, or doctrine is offered here.

Only structural distinctions and links are considered — those that remain invariant across changes of language, tradition, and interpretation.
